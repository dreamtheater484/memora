Privacy

Memora is a notebook app that runs on your own computer, or on a server of your own. Its makers run no service for it: nobody but you receives your notes, and Memora sends no telemetry.

Your notes

Memora keeps your notes on your computer (or your server). It connects to the internet only to check GitHub for a new version, to download the pictures of a web page you paste, and, when you turn sync on, to reach the sync folder you chose.

Sync through Google Drive

When you sync your computers through Google Drive, Memora asks Google for one permission: to see, edit, create and delete only the Google Drive files that Memora itself creates (the drive.file scope). With it, Google lets Memora see nothing else in your Drive.

  • Memora uses that access only to keep your notes the same on your own computers: it writes and reads its own files in its own folder, and removes old ones it no longer needs.
  • Everything it writes is encrypted on your computer first, with a passphrase only you know. Neither Google nor the makers of Memora can read it.
  • The sign-in stays on your computer, kept by the operating system's own protection. It is not sent anywhere but to Google.
  • Memora doesn't send data from your Google account to anyone else, and doesn't use it for anything but sync. Its use of data received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
  • To stop it, turn sync off in Memora (Settings → Sync), or remove Memora's access in your Google account. Memora's folder stays in your Drive until you delete it.

Sync through WebDAV or a folder

With kDrive, Nextcloud or another WebDAV server, Memora connects to the address you gave it, with the password you gave it, and works in that folder only. With a folder on your computer, Memora connects to nothing: your sync app does.

Questions

Memora is open source: everything it does can be read in its source code. Ask questions or report a problem on GitHub.
